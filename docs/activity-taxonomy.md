# Dewey 2.0 — Activity Taxonomy

Activities are the atomic units of work in a coaching plan. Every activity belongs to one of four categories. Category determines:
- Who or what decides the activity is "done"
- Whether AI is available within the activity
- The default gating mode (OPEN vs. REVIEWED)

---

## Category 1: Reflecting & Solving

**Completion determined by:** AI judgment (within the activity) or coach review  
**AI available:** Yes — this is the primary AI-involved activity type  
**Default gating:** REVIEWED (coach sees the conversation and approves)

These are structured AI conversations. The partner works through a problem, goal, or reflection with the AI as a Socratic thinking partner. The AI guides the conversation through a defined objective and signals when completion criteria are met, but the coach reviews before the activity is marked done.

**Examples:**
| Activity | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Describe Current Reality | Partner articulates the current state of a problem or practice gap |
| Develop a Goal | Partner works toward a specific, measurable improvement goal |
| Identify Root Cause | Partner surfaces and tests hypotheses about why a problem persists |
| Define Conditions for Success | Partner articulates what solving the problem looks like concretely |
| Interrogate an Assumption | Partner examines a belief or mental model that may be limiting their approach |
| Navigate a Stakeholder Situation | Partner maps stakeholders and develops an influence strategy |
| Take a Perspective | Partner builds understanding of another person's viewpoint |
| Reflect on a Measured Outcome | Partner examines results and decides whether to declare success, refine, or revisit |

---

## Category 2: Input & Inquiry

**Completion determined by:** Partner self-attestation  
**AI available:** No (optional reflection prompt may be surfaced, but no conversation)  
**Default gating:** OPEN

The partner does something — reads, analyzes, observes, interviews — and marks it complete when they've done it. The activity may produce an artifact (notes, a written reflection, a data summary). Completion is on the partner's honor; the coach can see the artifact if one was produced.

**Examples:**
| Activity | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Read a Text | Partner reads an assigned article, chapter, or book section |
| Analyze Data | Partner examines student performance, observation, or other data |
| Interview a Stakeholder | Partner conducts a structured conversation to gather perspective |
| Observe a Classroom | Partner conducts or attends a classroom observation |
| Review Student Work | Partner examines student artifacts against a standard or rubric |
| Survey Your Team | Partner gathers structured input from colleagues |
| Research a Strategy | Partner investigates an approach or intervention before trying it |

---

## Category 3: Observation & Being Observed

**Completion determined by:** Coach  
**AI available:** No  
**Default gating:** REVIEWED (coach marks complete after the observation occurs)

These activities involve live or recorded observation — either the coach observing the partner, or the partner observing themselves or peers. Completion requires the coach to confirm the observation happened and was substantive enough to count.

**Examples:**
| Activity | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Host Coach's Observation | Coach observes the partner's practice in person or via recording |
| Record and Review Own Practice | Partner records themselves and reviews the recording |
| Conduct a Peer Observation | Partner observes a colleague's practice |
| Participate in a Learning Walk | Partner participates in a structured school walkthrough |

---

## Category 4: Implementation

**Completion determined by:** Coach  
**AI available:** No  
**Default gating:** REVIEWED (coach marks complete after confirming implementation occurred)

The partner does something in practice — tries a strategy, co-teaches, facilitates a session. These are real-world actions, not reflections or observations. The coach confirms it happened.

**Examples:**
| Activity | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Try a Strategy | Partner implements a specific instructional or leadership strategy |
| Co-Teach a Session | Partner and coach co-deliver instruction |
| Facilitate a Meeting | Partner leads a team, PLG, or staff meeting with an intentional structure |
| Deliver Professional Development | Partner designs and delivers PD to their team |
| Implement a Plan | Partner executes a multi-step action plan developed in coaching |

---

## Done States vs. Phase Exit Conditions

These are distinct concepts:

- **Done state** — how we know a specific activity is complete. Determined by category (see above). Applies at the activity level.
- **Exit condition** — the criteria evaluated at the *phase* level when all activities are complete. Asks "has the partner done enough work in this phase to advance?" Evaluated by AI, surfaced to coach, approved by coach.

An activity being "done" does not automatically mean its phase is ready to exit. Exit conditions may require a pattern of evidence across multiple activities.

---

## Gating Override

While each category has a default gating mode, coaches can override per-activity when building a plan:

- A Category 2 (Input & Inquiry) activity can be set to REVIEWED if the coach wants to see the partner's notes before it counts
- A Category 4 (Implementation) activity could theoretically be set to OPEN if the coach trusts self-attestation for that specific context

The default is set by category; the override is set per-activity in the plan builder.
